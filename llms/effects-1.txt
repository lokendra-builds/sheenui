### About effects

#### Overview

These are the animated React components in SheenUI: glassy, classy effects you drop onto a page to make it feel alive. Each of the 19 pages below has a live demo, the props, the keyboard and reduced-motion behavior, and the files to copy. They are built with Motion (formerly Framer Motion), the same engine that powers the rest of the library, so one provider sets the spring and the pace for everything.

The set mixes surface effects and text effects. [Liquid Glass for React](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/liquid-glass) bends the background behind a panel, the way iOS 26 does, with a frosted fallback for Safari and Firefox. A [border beam](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/border-beam) sends light around a card, a [spotlight card](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/spotlight-card) follows the pointer, and the [macOS-style dock](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/macos-dock) magnifies icons as you move across them. For text there are a [number ticker](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/number-ticker), a [typewriter](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/typewriter), [shimmer text](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/shimmer-text) and a [blur-in](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/blur-in) reveal.

Which effect for which job: for text, [typewriter](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/typewriter), [word rotate](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/word-rotate), [shimmer text](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/shimmer-text) and [text reveal](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/text-reveal); for a background, [particles](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/particles) and [meteors](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/meteors); for a card, [3D tilt](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/3d-tilt-card), [spotlight](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/spotlight-card) and [border beam](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/border-beam); for a diagram, [animated beam](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/animated-beam) and [orbiting circles](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/orbiting-circles); for a success moment, [confetti](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/confetti).

By how they move: [particles](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/particles), [meteors](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/meteors), [marquee](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/marquee) and [orbiting circles](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/orbiting-circles) loop on their own; [spotlight](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/spotlight-card), [3D tilt](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/3d-tilt-card) and the [dock](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/macos-dock) answer the pointer; [scroll progress](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/scroll-progress) answers the page scroll; [confetti](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/confetti) and [morphing dialog](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/effects/morphing-dialog) play once when you trigger them. Pick the least continuous motion that does the job, because a loop is the only kind that keeps running while someone reads.

What every effect shares. One SheenMotionProvider at the root of your app wraps Motion’s config: it makes Motion follow the operating system’s reduced-motion setting and SheenUI’s own switch, sets the default spring, pauses looping CSS animations while they are off screen and pauses decorative loops in a hidden tab. Timing comes from shared tokens, with durations between 0.1 and 0.6 seconds, an exit that is always shorter than the entrance and never overshoots, so a toast and a dialog feel like one product.

To use one, open its page and copy the files it lists, which include the motion tokens that effect imports, or install it through the shadcn registry as the [installation page](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/docs/installation) shows, after the [Tailwind CSS v4 setup](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/docs/tailwind-v4). The effects are plain React source in your repository, so you can change a spring or a duration without waiting for a release.

Motion that ignores people is a bug, so every effect respects reduced motion and pauses when it leaves the screen. Learn the patterns in [12 Framer Motion examples](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/guides/framer-motion-examples), or read why the package was renamed in [Framer Motion is now Motion](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/guides/framer-motion-to-motion). For the static building blocks see [all glass UI components](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/components/), and for full screens the [React blocks](https://lokendra-builds.github.io/sheenui/blocks/).

#### Frequently asked questions

#### Which animation library do the effects use?

Motion, the library formerly called Framer Motion, imported from motion/react. Springs and durations come from shared motion tokens so every effect feels the same.

#### What happens with reduced motion?

Each effect has a still fallback: movement turns into a short fade or the final state is shown at once, whether the setting comes from the operating system or from SheenUI's own toggle.

#### Can I use one effect without the rest of SheenUI?

Yes. Each effect page lists the files to copy; they need Motion (motion/react), Tailwind CSS v4 and the SheenUI tokens and motion presets they import. Add SheenMotionProvider at the root as well: without it Motion ignores the operating system's reduced-motion setting.

#### Do the effects hurt performance?

They animate transform and opacity, pause when off screen or in a hidden tab, and a page keeps to a budget of 8 blurred surfaces in view. See the backdrop-filter performance guide for the glass cost.

### Props

| Prop | Type | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| text | string | required | The text to reveal. |
| by | "word" \| "character" | "word" | Reveal word by word (default), or character by character (also the choice for languages written without spaces). |
| delay | number | 0 | Seconds before the first piece starts. |
| stagger | number | 0.07 for words, 0.025 for characters | Sets the seconds between pieces. |
| duration | number | durations.slower | Seconds each piece takes to arrive. |
| blur | number | 10 | Starting blur in px. 0 for a plain fade-up. |
| offset | string | "0.35em" | How far each piece rises, as a CSS length. "0" for none. |
| animateOnView | boolean | true | Wait until the text is on screen. Off: play on mount. |
| once | boolean | true | Play only the first time it enters the view. Off: replay every time it comes back. |
| onComplete | (() => void) | optional, no default | Called when the last piece has landed. |
| pieceClassName | string | optional, no default | Classes for each animated piece (e.g. a gradient per word). |
| render | ReactElement \| function | <span /> | Render as another element: an h1, a p… |

### Example 1 of 2: Hero headline

Render it as any element with render (here paragraphs styled as a headline; use render={<h1 />} for the page's real heading). It plays once, when it scrolls into view.

```tsx
import { BlurIn } from "@/components/sheenui/text-effects/blur-in";
// Package build (after the first npm release, or a packed tarball): import { BlurIn } from "@sheenui/theme";

export function Example() {
  return (
    <div className="flex max-w-2xl flex-col items-center gap-4 text-center">
      <BlurIn
        render={<p />}
        text="Interfaces you can see through."
        className="text-4xl leading-[1.08] font-semibold tracking-tight text-balance sm:text-5xl"
      />
      <BlurIn
        render={<p />}
        text="Glass components with motion that stays readable, accessible and fast."
        delay={0.45}
        stagger={0.03}
        blur={6}
        className="max-w-lg text-base text-pretty text-sheen-fg-muted sm:text-lg"
      />
    </div>
  );
}
```

### Example 2 of 2: Character by character

by="character" reveals each letter (emoji and accents stay whole). Words never break apart mid-line. It is also the mode for languages written without spaces.

```tsx
import { BlurIn } from "@/components/sheenui/text-effects/blur-in";
// Package build (after the first npm release, or a packed tarball): import { BlurIn } from "@sheenui/theme";

export function Example() {
  return (
    <div className="flex flex-col items-center gap-3 text-center">
      <BlurIn
        by="character"
        render={<p />}
        text="Ship it ✨"
        blur={12}
        offset="0.5em"
        className="text-5xl font-bold tracking-tight sm:text-6xl"
      />
      <BlurIn
        by="character"
        render={<p />}
        text="こんにちは、世界"
        delay={0.5}
        stagger={0.05}
        lang="ja"
        className="text-xl font-medium text-sheen-fg-muted"
      />
    </div>
  );
}
```

### Accessibility

- The text is in the DOM from the start, so screen readers and search engines read it once, whether or not it has animated.
- Under reduced motion it is plain static text, with nothing to wait for.

### When to use the blur in effect

- Use it as a text reveal animation on the one hero headline of a landing page, where each word sharpens out of a soft blur as it rises into place and gives the page a deliberate first impression.
- Use it for an empty-state or onboarding welcome line, such as a first-run greeting in a dashboard, where one short sentence should arrive with a little ceremony instead of simply appearing.
- Switch to by="character" for a short tagline or for languages written without spaces, each word stays one unbreakable box, so letters never wrap on their own.
- Skip it when the text is body copy, a list or anything people scan quickly; animated paragraphs slow reading, so keep plain text there or use Typewriter for a phrase that changes over time.
- Pair it with ShimmerText for a subtitle that lights up after the headline lands, a Button for the call to action, and the useSheenReducedMotion hook if you gate other motion yourself.


### Frequently asked questions

#### How do I make a blur text animation in React with BlurIn?

Pass the string as the text prop and choose the element with render, for example render={<h1 />}. Tune it with by, delay, stagger, duration, blur (starting px, default 10) and offset (default 0.35em). It plays once when the text scrolls into view.

#### Will screen readers hear the headline word by word?

No. A visually hidden copy of the whole string is read once, and the animated pieces are aria-hidden. That hidden copy is unselectable, so copying the headline gives exactly what is on screen, once, instead of duplicated text.

#### How do I replay the animation or run it on mount?

Set animateOnView={false} to play on mount instead of waiting for the viewport, and once={false} to replay each time it scrolls back in. To trigger it from a button, change the component key so it remounts. onComplete fires after the last piece lands.

#### What happens with reduced motion, and does it leave a filter on the text?

With the OS setting or the in-app toggle on, you get plain static text with nothing to wait for. When each piece lands its filter is removed, so finished text is ordinary text. Use pieceClassName to style every word, such as a gradient.

### Props

| Prop | Type | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| duration | number | 8 | Seconds one lap around the border takes. |
| delay | number | 0 | Seconds before the first lap (lets several beams run out of step). |
| length | number | 120 | Length of the beam in px, measured along the border. |
| width | number | 1.5 | Sets the thickness of the ring in px. |
| colorFrom | string | "var(--sheen-accent)" | Sets the tail colour (any CSS colour). |
| colorTo | string | "var(--sheen-accent-2)" | Colour of the beam's bright head, any CSS colour; the tail fades in from colorFrom. |
| reverse | boolean | false | Runs the beam anticlockwise instead of clockwise. |
| double | boolean | false | Draws two beams on opposite sides. |
| glow | boolean | true | Soft halo around the beam (skipped for lite glass and reduced transparency). It moves by transform; nothing is blurred per frame. |
| track | boolean | true | Faint, static rim under the beam, so the border reads as lit even between laps. |
| offset | number | -1 | Distance from the parent's padding edge in px. -1 sits on a 1px border. |
| paused | boolean | false | Hold the beam where it is (it carries on from there when unpaused). |
| className | string | optional, no default | Adds extra classes to the beam element. |
| style | CSSProperties | optional, no default | Inline style for the beam element (for example custom properties). |

### Example 1 of 2: On a glass card

Drop it inside any positioned, rounded element: it takes the parent's radius and sits on its border. paused holds the beam exactly where it is.

```tsx
import { useState } from "react";

import { GlassCard } from "@/components/sheenui/glass-card/glass-card";
import { Switch } from "@/components/sheenui/switch/switch";
// Package build (after the first npm release, or a packed tarball): import { BorderBeam, GlassCard, Switch } from "@sheenui/theme";

export function Example() {
  const [paused, setPaused] = useState(false);

  return (
    <>
      <Switch label="Pause the beam" checked={paused} onCheckedChange={setPaused} />
      <GlassCard className="relative w-full max-w-sm p-6">
        <h3 id="deploying-to-production" className="font-semibold">Deploying to production</h3>
        <p className="text-sm text-sheen-fg-muted">Build 2f9c1a · 14 of 18 steps</p>
        <BorderBeam paused={paused} />
```

### Accessibility

- Purely decorative: aria-hidden, no focus, no pointer events, and hidden in forced colors.
- It sits on a card you provide, so contrast is that of the card. Under reduced motion it is a still beam lit on the top-left corner.

### When to use the border beam effect

- Use it on the recommended plan of a pricing table or one featured card in a grid, so a single comet of light circles that edge while its neighbours stay still.
- Use it on a focused input wrapper or a primary call-to-action button to show an AI prompt box is live or that a card is selected, without adding a heavy glow.
- Use double and reverse to make a featured tile look different from its neighbors, and delay to stagger several beams so they never run in lockstep.
- Skip it when the surface is not a rounded element you control or you need a static highlight; a plain border, or SpotlightCard for a hover-driven glow, fits better than a looping animation.
- Pair it with GlassCard as the parent, Badge for a Recommended label, and Button inside the card so the beam draws attention to the action rather than competing with it.


### Frequently asked questions

#### How do I add a border beam in React?

Place BorderBeam as a child of a positioned, rounded element, such as a GlassCard with relative and a radius class. It fills its parent, takes the parent's border radius and is masked to a thin ring, so it never covers content or touches the parent's blur.

#### Which props change the beam's speed, size and colors?

duration is seconds per lap (default 8), length is the beam length in px (120), and width is the ring thickness (1.5). colorFrom is the tail and colorTo the head, defaulting to the accent and teal. Also available: reverse, double, glow, track, offset and delay.

#### Is it accessible, and does it hurt performance?

It is purely decorative: aria-hidden, not focusable, no pointer events, and hidden in forced colors. It pauses while off screen, and the halo moves by transform with nothing blurred per frame. The paused prop holds it in place until you release it.

#### What does the border beam do with reduced motion, and how does it handle a wide bar?

Under reduced motion it shows a still beam lit on the top-left corner. The keyframes follow the real outline, so on a wide bar the beam keeps one speed and one length all the way round instead of racing along the long sides like a spinning gradient.

### When to use the macOS dock effect

- Use it as a floating dock with magnification for an app launcher or quick-action bar in a desktop-style web app, where icons near the pointer swell and push neighbors apart like the macOS Dock.

### Frequently asked questions

#### How do I build a macOS dock in React with SheenUI?

Wrap DockItem children in Dock and give the dock a label. Each DockItem needs a label and an icon, plus onClick or href. Use size for the resting icon size (default 48), magnification from 1 to 2 (default 1.6) and distance for how far the swell reaches.

#### How do keyboard users and screen readers work with the dock?

It is a Base UI toolbar: one Tab stop, with arrow keys, Home and End moving between items. The focused item magnifies and shows its label; Escape hides the label. Each control carries its label as its accessible name, and active links get aria-current="page".

#### What happens on touch screens or when there is no room to magnify?

The dock falls back to a plain bar on touch screens and coarse pointers, under reduced motion, and when there is too little room beside it. With limited room the swell is scaled down to fit. If the bar is wider than its container it scrolls inside itself.

#### How do I mark an item as open or show a badge?

Set active on a DockItem to draw a dot under the icon. Links get aria-current, and buttons add labels.active to their name, such as Finder, open. The badge prop shows a count on the corner, and badgeLabel sets what screen readers hear, like 3 unread.

### Example 1 of 3: Drag the lens

A clear circle lens over crisp artwork. Drag it (or focus it and use the arrow keys) and watch the lines bend and split into color at the rim.

```tsx
import { useRef, type KeyboardEvent } from "react";
import { LiquidGlass } from "@/components/sheenui/liquid-glass/liquid-glass";
// Package build (after the first npm release, or a packed tarball): import { LiquidGlass } from "@sheenui/theme";
import { motion, useMotionValue } from "motion/react";

const moves: Partial<Record<string, [number, number]>> = { ArrowLeft: [-1, 0], ArrowRight: [1, 0], ArrowUp: [0, -1], ArrowDown: [0, 1] };
// Written out here on purpose: the library's clamp is internal, and this example has to run on its own.
const clamp = (v: number, min: number, max: number) => Math.max(min, Math.min(max, v));

export function Example() {
  const areaRef = useRef<HTMLDivElement>(null);
  const x = useMotionValue(0);
  const y = useMotionValue(0);

  // Arrow keys move the lens (Shift for bigger steps), kept inside the artwork.
  function nudge(event: KeyboardEvent<HTMLDivElement>) {
    const move = moves[event.key];
    const area = areaRef.current;
    if (!move || !area) return;
    event.preventDefault();
    const lens = event.currentTarget;
    const step = event.shiftKey ? 64 : 16;
    x.set(clamp(x.get() + move[0] * step, -lens.offsetLeft, area.clientWidth - lens.offsetLeft - lens.offsetWidth));
    y.set(clamp(y.get() + move[1] * step, -lens.offsetTop, area.clientHeight - lens.offsetTop - lens.offsetHeight));
  }

  return (
    <div ref={areaRef} className="relative h-96 overflow-hidden rounded-sheen-xl bg-gradient-to-br from-fuchsia-500 via-violet-500 to-sky-400">
      <LiquidGlass
        shape="circle"
        variant="clear"
        aberration={0.6}
        render={
          <motion.div
            drag
            dragConstraints={areaRef}
            dragElastic={0.12}
            whileDrag={{ scale: 1.06 }}
            style={{ x, y }}
          />
        }
        tabIndex={0}
        role="group"
        aria-roledescription="movable lens"
        aria-label="Lens. Drag it, or use the arrow keys to move it."
        onKeyDown={nudge}
        className="absolute top-1/2 left-1/2 -mt-20 -ml-20 size-40 cursor-grab touch-none"
      />
    </div>
  );
}
```

### When to use the liquid glass effect

- Use it as a liquid glass effect for a few hero surfaces that sit over photos or video, such as a floating media control, a caption overlay or a navigation pill that visibly bends the page behind it in Chromium.

### Frequently asked questions

#### How do I use liquid glass in React, and does it work in every browser?

Render LiquidGlass with a shape and your content. The real refraction runs in Chromium through an SVG filter on backdrop-filter. Safari and Firefox draw nothing for that filter, so they get SheenUI frosted glass instead. supportsLiquidGlass() tells you which one you got.

#### Why do only some of my lenses bend the background?

Refraction has a page-wide budget of three lenses near the viewport at once; the rest render as frosted glass until a slot frees up, and a lens that scrolls away hands over its slot. Treat it as an accent for hero surfaces, not a card style.

#### Which props tune the look of the lens?

variant is regular (tinted, for text) or clear. refraction runs from 0 to 2 with a default of 1, bezel sets the rim width, frost the blur, saturation the color boost (1.5), aberration splits edge colors from 0 to 1, and specular toggles the bright rim.

#### Is text on a lens readable, and what about reduced transparency?

Text on a regular lens is designed to keep 4.5:1 contrast on any backdrop. With reduced transparency, more contrast or no backdrop-filter support you get a solid surface, forced colors use system colors, and no displacement map is drawn. Only scale ever animates.

### Example 2 of 3: Testimonials in glass

Level-1 glass items (tint, rim and light, no blur of their own) keep a moving strip cheap: a blurred card re-blurs its backdrop on every frame it moves, and a dozen in view would blow the glass budget. Put a blurred level (glass-2) on the items and the edges fade card by card instead, each card masked on its own glass element so its blur keeps working (a mask on the strip would switch it off). Both rows share one Pause button.

```tsx
];

    <figure>
      <blockquote className="text-sm">{text}</blockquote>

}
```

### Accessibility

- Mouse over it, or keyboard focus inside it, eases it to a stop (and back up after); focusing an off-screen item glides it into view.
- The strip has a Pause / Play button (WCAG 2.2.2). The list is rendered once for assistive tech; the repeated copies are aria-hidden and out of the tab order. listLabel names the list.
- Under reduced motion it does not move, and the copies, the edge fade and the button are dropped.

### All exports on this page

- Marquee — Marquee: an endless, seamless row (or column) of logos, testimonials, anything.
- defaultMarqueeLabels — The English MarqueeLabels used when labels is omitted; a partial labels prop is merged over it.


### When to use the marquee effect

- Use it for a Trusted by strip of customer logos below a landing page hero, or a row of short testimonials, that scrolls on its own at a steady pace.
- Use it for a review wall or testimonial rows, running two marquees in opposite directions with reverse, or a vertical column with vertical and a fixed height for a sidebar of quotes.
- Pass itemClassName with a glass class to turn every item into a glass card; the edge fade is then applied item by item so the backdrop blur keeps working.
- Skip it when the content is long, must be read carefully or contains important actions; moving content competes for attention, so use a static grid where users control the pace.
- Pair it with Avatar and Badge for testimonial cards, GlassCard for each item, and a Switch or your own button wired to the paused prop to control several marquees at once.


### Frequently asked questions

#### How do I make a react marquee that never jumps?

Give Marquee children, one per item, and set speed in px per second (default 40). The list is copied as many times as needed to fill the strip, and the loop distance is one copy plus one gap, so it repeats seamlessly. gap, reverse and fade adjust spacing, direction and edges.

#### Is a moving marquee accessible?

A Pause / Play button sits below the strip and comes first in the tab order, which covers WCAG 2.2.2; Enter or Space toggles it. The list is rendered once for assistive tech, named by listLabel, while the copies are aria-hidden and out of the tab order.

#### How do I pause it on hover or from my own control?

Hover or keyboard focus inside eases the strip to a stop, and pauseOnHover={false} keeps it running under the mouse. For external control use paused with onPausedChange, which lets one button drive several marquees. defaultPaused starts it stopped.

#### What happens with reduced motion or right-to-left layouts?

With reduced motion it does not move: a row wraps onto as many lines as it needs, a column keeps its height and scrolls, and there are no copies, fade or button. In right-to-left pages the row starts from the right and moves the other way.

- The card is a native button with aria-haspopup="dialog"; the dialog is role="dialog", labeled by MorphingDialogTitle and described by MorphingDialogDescription. Always render a title.
- Controls go in MorphingDialogActions, which fades each one in after the flight.
- Under reduced motion the flight is removed and the dialog only fades; describe informative media with alt.

### All exports on this page

- MorphingDialog — Morphing dialog: a card that grows into a dialog.
- MorphingDialogActions — The dialog's buttons and other controls (a row by default).
- MorphingDialogClose — The × button (added automatically by MorphingDialogContent unless showClose={false}).
- MorphingDialogContent — The dialog.
- MorphingDialogDescription — Text that only exists in the dialog.
- MorphingDialogMedia — The picture (an <img>, a video poster, artwork) that flies from the card into the dialog.
- MorphingDialogSubtitle — A line under the title (place, author, price) that also moves from the card into the dialog.
- MorphingDialogTitle — The title.
- MorphingDialogTrigger — The card.
- defaultMorphingDialogLabels — The English MorphingDialogLabels used when labels is omitted; a partial labels prop is merged over it.


### When to use the morphing dialog effect

- Use it as a card to modal animation for gallery and product cards that open into detail, where the picture, title and subtitle fly from the card into the dialog instead of a modal appearing from nowhere.
- Use it for a portfolio grid, a travel destination list or a recipe index, composing MorphingDialogTrigger with MorphingDialogContent and the shared Media, Title, Subtitle, Description and Actions pieces.
- Keep the media's aspect ratio the same in the card and in the dialog, otherwise the shared image stretches during the flight and the morph looks unclean.
- Skip it when the dialog is a form or a confirmation with no card to grow from; use Dialog for those, since a morph only makes sense when the trigger visibly becomes the content.
- Pair it with Button inside MorphingDialogActions so each control fades itself, Badge for a category label on the card, and GlassCard grids to lay the triggers out.


### Frequently asked questions

#### How do I build a morphing dialog in React?

Wrap MorphingDialogTrigger and MorphingDialogContent in MorphingDialog. Put the same MorphingDialogMedia, Title and Subtitle in both so they share layout ids and fly into place, then add Description and Actions in the content. Use open and onOpenChange for control, or defaultOpen.

#### How do focus and the keyboard work?

Enter or Space on the card opens it; focus moves in and is trapped, Escape closes, and focus returns to the card. Tab and Shift+Tab are handled by the dialog through every control, which keeps the trap exact in Safari. It is built on the Base UI Dialog.

#### What does the trigger render, and how do I style it?

The trigger is a native button with aria-haspopup="dialog", named by its text. It accepts level (1 to 3, default 2) for the glass, tint for an accent surface and radius in px, which defaults to the theme's --sheen-radius-lg. Radii are scale-corrected so corners stay round mid-flight.

#### What happens with reduced motion, and why use MorphingDialogActions?

With reduced motion nothing flies: the dialog simply fades in over the card and out again. Controls belong in MorphingDialogActions because each one fades itself; wrapping a glass button in a faded wrapper would switch its backdrop blur off while it appears.

### When to use the number ticker effect

- Use it for the headline figure of a KPI tile or a totals row that is recalculated when someone picks another date range or filter, so the change reads as motion instead of a jump.
- Use it for a price or revenue figure with currency formatting, such as 48,250 dollars with no decimals, or a stat row on a landing page that counts up when it scrolls into view.

### Frequently asked questions

#### How do I build a number ticker in React with currency formatting?

#### Which direction do the digits roll?

By default they roll up when the value rises and down when it falls. trend="up" or "down" forces a direction. Each digit keeps its place when the number grows or shrinks, so new places slide in instead of everything jumping, and large changes spin an extra turn.

#### How do screen readers hear the changing number?

The wheels are aria-hidden, and the final value sits in a visually hidden live region, read once and correctly formatted rather than digit by digit. live defaults to "polite", can be "assertive", and "off" suits numbers that change constantly. prefix and suffix are read too.

#### When does the first roll start, and what about reduced motion?

It rolls from from to value the first time it scrolls into view; animateOnView={false} rolls on mount, and delay and duration tune that first roll. With reduced motion the value simply changes with no rolling. It uses transforms and fades, with no backdrop blur.

#### How do I add a shimmer text animation in React?

The sweep is transform only, using native Web Animations on two transforms, with no per-frame paint. A looping shimmer pauses off screen and in hidden tabs, and paused stops it from outside. The overlay is clipped sideways so it can never widen the page.

With reduced motion there is no overlay at all, just the text. In forced colors the overlay is hidden. Avoid interactive content in children, because the content is copied for the highlight layer and a duplicate control would be confusing.

### Accessibility

- No role of its own; reading order is the DOM order. SpotlightGroup shares one pointer listener across the cards.

### When to use the spotlight card effect

- Use it as a cursor glow effect for a feature grid on a landing page, where a soft glow follows the pointer across each glass card and the rim lights up as the cursor nears an edge.
- Use it for pricing tiles or integration cards in a dashboard, with tint to give one accent-colored tile more weight than its neighbors.
- Wrap neighboring cards in SpotlightGroup so a card's rim picks up the light even while the pointer is still over the next card, as on Linear-style grids.
- Skip it when you want a calm surface or a card that physically leans: use GlassCard for static panels and TiltCard for 3D movement toward the pointer, since glow adds noise to dense lists.
- Pair it with SpotlightGroup for the grid, Badge for a plan label, and Button or Switch inside the cards, which keep their own focus rings while the glow reacts to focus.


### Frequently asked questions

#### How do I make a spotlight card in React?

Render SpotlightCard like a div with padding. level (1 to 3, default 2) and tint pick the glass; size sets the glow diameter in px, defaulting to 90 percent of the card width up to 320px. color sets the glow, rimColor the edge light, and rim={false} removes the rim.

#### How does it avoid re-rendering on every mouse move?

The pointer position is handled at most once per frame, with no React state, and written straight to the glow layers. Those layers are pre-painted gradients moved with translate, so a pointer move never repaints the card or touches its backdrop blur.

#### What happens on touch screens and with the keyboard?

Tracking is mouse and pen only, because a finger dragging across a card is scrolling. Without hover the glow stays static near the top. A plain card is not focusable, but keyboard focus inside it lights it, so interactive children still get the glow.

#### Is it accessible, and what about reduced motion?

The glow and rim are aria-hidden and hidden in forced colors. Text contrast is measured against the glass, not the glow. Reduced motion removes the fades. The card is one backdrop-filter surface, and the glow layers sit inside it behind your content.

### When to use the 3D tilt card effect

- Use it on a hero tile, a pricing card or a product card that should feel physical under the pointer: it tilts in 3D, and the glare and rim light slide across the glass as it moves.
- Use it for a pricing card or a profile showcase, wrapping the title in TiltCardLayer with a depth so it drifts toward the pointer and appears to float above the surface.
- Tune the motion with maxTilt (0 to 8 degrees), hoverScale and perspective, and set glare={false} when you want the lean without the light.
- Skip it when the card is large, dense or full of controls; a flat glow from SpotlightCard or a calm GlassCard is easier to read, and tilting a form makes it harder to aim.

### Frequently asked questions

#### How do I build a 3D tilt card in React?

Render TiltCard with padding and optionally maxTilt, a value from 0 to 8 degrees with a default of 6. hoverScale defaults to 1.02, and perspective defaults to 1000 px, where a smaller number is more dramatic. level and tint choose the glass, like other cards.

#### Does tilting break the glass blur?

No. The tilt is applied to the glass element itself, as a perspective, rotateX and rotateY transform, never to a wrapper, so the backdrop blur keeps working. TiltCardLayer fakes depth without preserve-3d, which glass cannot use, by drifting toward the pointer.

#### Why does the card not shake when the pointer rests near a corner?

Whether the pointer is on the card is decided against the card's untilted box, not the tilted shape. A tilted corner pulls in a few pixels, and testing against the moving shape would make the card jitter. It also eases into the lean even when entering at an edge.

#### What happens on touch and with reduced motion?

Tilt is off for touch, since a finger on a card is scrolling, and under reduced motion; you then get a still glass card. The glare, streak and rim are aria-hidden and hidden in forced colors, and the glare sits behind your content so text stays crisp.

### When to use the typewriter effect

- Use it for the rotating tagline in a portfolio or landing page hero, where one line has to say several things in turn: each phrase is typed out, held long enough to read, then deleted.
- Use it for a terminal-style intro or a demo of a search box suggestion, where one phrase types once with the caret blinking and fading at the end.
- Use typeSpeed, deleteSpeed, hold and startDelay to tune the rhythm; typing already has uneven keystrokes and a beat after punctuation, so it reads as human.
- Skip it when only a single word changes inside a sentence, where WordRotate slides words in a fixed slot, or when the text matters for scanning; BlurIn is better for a one-time headline reveal.
- Pair it with Button for the call to action below the line, ShimmerText for a supporting status line, and reserveSpace so the layout under the text never moves.


### Frequently asked questions

#### How do I create a typewriter effect in React?

Pass words as a string or an array to Typewriter. With several phrases, loop defaults to true; with one it types once and onComplete fires after the last phrase. typeSpeed defaults to 0.055 seconds per character, deleteSpeed to 0.028, hold to 1.8 and startDelay to 0.2.

#### Does the text jump around while it types?

Not by default. reserveSpace is true, so the space of the longest phrase is reserved up front and nothing around it moves. Characters are grapheme clusters through Intl.Segmenter, so emoji and accents never split in the middle of a character.

#### What do screen readers read, and can people stop it?

Screen readers get every phrase once as plain text, or whatever you pass in label; the typing itself is aria-hidden. It pauses while the tab is hidden or the pointer rests on it, and the paused prop pauses it from your own button, for WCAG 2.2.2.

#### How does the caret behave, and what is the reduced-motion result?

The caret is solid while typing and blinks while waiting, and solid and still when paused. Style it with cursorClassName or hide it with cursor={false}. With reduced motion you get the first phrase, complete, with no caret and nothing to wait for.
